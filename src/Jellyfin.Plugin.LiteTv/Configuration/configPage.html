LiteTV Channels

Channel viewing is recorded against this Jellyfin account instead of yours, which is what keeps it out of Continue Watching, Next Up and the watched flags. The account is created on first use, hidden from the login screen, and can do nothing but play. Leave it as it is unless the name clashes with a real account.

Two things worth knowing. Anyone who can reach this server's API can ask for this account's token, so on a server with several people and libraries not everyone may see, restrict this account to match in Dashboard → Users. And the same title watched deliberately while a channel is on will not be recorded either — it is the account that decides, not what is playing.
Uses SponsorBlock's public database to seek past an uploader's branded card, a plea to subscribe, or a read for something unrelated — the things German trailer channels wrap a trailer in, and which a minute-long break has no room for. The lookup asks about a four-character hash of the video id rather than the id itself, so the service is never told what is playing; switching this off stops the request being made at all.
What YouTube hands over depends on who is asking, and it differs by client, by day, and by whether the asker looks like a real device — Android VR gives every rendition unobfuscated to a phone running microG, and answers "login required" to a server with no Google identity to offer. This is the one thing about trailers that cannot be settled from here, so it can be pinned and tried from the television. Leave it on "try them all" unless you are testing.

Channels

This week

The channel's schedule, written down. A week is the whole of it: what is here airs this week, next week and every week after. Drag a bar to move it, drag its lower edge to make it longer, drop something from the shelf below onto an empty stretch to schedule it. Anything you drop holds its own time and what was there bends around it.

The shelf

What you can drop onto the week. Films and episodes bring their own runtime; an advert or an address is as long as you say it is.
Paste an address and its length is worked out.

Program (looping forever)

Program blocks

Parts of the week that air something else — the kids' programming until noon, the film on Saturday evening. Whatever no block covers airs the channel's own program above. A block picks up where it left off the last time it was on.
Click a bar to edit it, drag it to move it, drag its right edge to make it longer or shorter. Click an empty stretch to start a block there. Everything snaps to a quarter of an hour. Thin marks are trailer slots.
May run past midnight; it then continues into the following day.
Days the block starts on. None ticked means every day.

Adverts in the breaks

Addresses the client resolves and plays, exactly as it does a linked trailer — a YouTube link to an old advert is the intended use. They go at the front of a break and the trailer goes last, so a break ends on what the channel is about to show. A decade is preferred over the pool at large when it matches the year the trailed programme was made, which is the whole joke; leave it at 0 for one that fits anywhere. Nothing is reported to Jellyfin — an advert is not library content and has no item to record against.
Length is worked out from the address.

Trailers

A trailer becomes part of the schedule like any other program, so it shows in the guide and everything after it moves along by its length. This is separate from filling slot gaps above — a channel running back to back has no gaps, and would otherwise never show one.
1 announces whatever is next; a few places ahead previews something still to come. The queue loops, so the last programs advertise what comes round at the start again.

Channel artwork

What a client draws the channel with. Left empty, a channel wears whatever it is airing — which suits a channel built from one series and does not suit one built from a genre, where what is on changes every hour and half of it has no wide picture at all. Pick from the channel's own content, paste an address, or upload a file.
Follows the title rather than copying it: re-scrape the series and the channel picks up the new artwork with it. Anything set above still wins.
Search any title, then pick one of its pictures. The channel need not air it — a poster is a poster.
Crop
Drag inside the picture to move the frame, and use the slider to zoom. The frame is the shape the slot wants, so what you see is what the television will show — nothing is cropped for you afterwards. Zoom out past the point where the picture fills the frame and the space around it is filled with a blurred copy of the picture, so a whole title still fits.

With 30, a program starts only on the hour or the half hour and the rest of its slot is time to fill — which is what lets a block promise the film at 20:15. A program longer than one slot takes as many whole slots as it needs.
Trailers held in the library air as ordinary schedule entries. Trailers the library only links to (usually YouTube) are not played yet.

App updates

This server can hand the television its own app updates, so a new build is uploaded here instead of being pushed over ADB — and ADB can then be switched off on the box. Put the address below into the app's Settings → Advanced → Update URL once; after that it checks here by itself and offers whatever build is newer than the one installed.
