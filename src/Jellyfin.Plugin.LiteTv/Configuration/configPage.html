What this channel plays
A series is every episode of it, a collection is everything in it. The order here is the order they are laid out in.
Blocks
A block is a stretch of the day with content of its own — a children's afternoon, a late film. Drawn on the week below; drag one to move it. Blocks only shape a week that is laid out afresh.
Adverts
Addresses the television resolves and plays. They go at the front of a break with the trailer last, so a break ends on what the channel is about to show. There is no length to type: the plugin asks how long the video is and which parts get skipped, and sizes the break by what is left.
Trailers
What a break advertises, and how often one happens.
A trailer is aired in a break for something the channel is about to show. Almost every trailer a library holds is a YouTube link rather than a file; the server resolves it to a stream the television can play, and skips the parts that are not the trailer.
The programme a break advertises is not the next one — it is the one that many places ahead, the way a channel trails the film after this one. Set it to 1 and a break advertises whatever follows it.

Only affects a channel with no week of its own.
A computed schedule is worked out from a fixed point in time, so the same channel shows the same thing at the same moment on every client. Moving that point to now restarts the rotation from its beginning. A channel with a stored week ignores this entirely — its week is the schedule.
Remove this channel
Takes the channel and its stored week away. Nothing in your library is touched — a channel is only ever a schedule over titles you already have.
These only shape a new week
Once a week is laid out it is the schedule, and nothing here rewrites it. Change these, then lay the week out again if you want them applied.
Episodes at a time is how many of a series run before the channel moves to its next source — two of a sitcom, then a film. Zero plays each source through in full.
Zero plays everything back to back.
Real channels start things on the half hour. A grid rounds each programme up to the next slot, and the gap it leaves is the break — which is where adverts and trailers go. Without a grid there are no gaps, and so no breaks at all.
Off means the channel sits silent until the next slot.

Where channels play
Channel viewing is recorded against this account, never yours.
Which is what keeps channels out of Continue Watching, Next Up and the watched flags. The account is created on first use, hidden from the login screen, and can do nothing but play.

Two things worth knowing. Anyone who can reach this server's API can ask for this account's token, so on a server with several people and libraries not everyone may see, restrict this account to match in Dashboard → Users. And the same title watched deliberately while a channel is on will not be recorded either — it is the account that decides, not what is playing.
Trailers
A branded card, a plea to subscribe, a read for something else.
Uses SponsorBlock's public database. The lookup asks about a four-character hash of the video id rather than the id itself, so the service is never told what is playing; switching this off stops the request being made at all. The break is sized by what is left after the skipping, which is why no length has to be typed anywhere.
Leave it on "try them all" unless you are testing.
What YouTube hands over depends on who is asking, and it differs by client, by day, and by whether the asker looks like a real device. This is the one thing about trailers that cannot be settled from here, so it can be pinned and tried from the television.
